What it does

Built for the person who runs the place

Not another CRM. Open it in the morning, see the day, close it.

◷

A calendar that reads like a calendar

One bar per booking, so you can see where a stay starts and ends. The period summary tells you how full you are, how many arrivals and departures there are, and what is still owed.

◈

Prices that change with the season

Set a season rate once and a discount for longer stays. The booking dialog shows how the total was reached, line by line, instead of a single number you have to trust.

✎

Contracts and invoices as real PDFs

A tenancy agreement for long lets, an invoice for any booking. Both print the figures stored with the booking, not today's prices, so an old document still matches what was agreed.

⇄

Works offline, syncs later

Your data lives on your machine. When the connection comes back it catches up with the server, and anything two devices disagreed about is shown to you as a list rather than quietly overwritten.

◐

Readable, light or dark

Light and dark themes, and a text size control for people who would rather not squint. Contrast is measured on every screen rather than eyeballed once.

⌂

Long lets next to short stays

A twelve month tenancy and a two night booking sit in the same calendar and the same availability check, so the two can never disagree about whether a property is free.

The calendar

Understand the day in one look

The grid answers which nights are taken. The two questions it used to leave you counting squares for are answered next to it: how full the period is, and when each property is next free.

  • One bar per booking, spanning the nights it holds
  • Occupancy, arrivals, departures and the balance for the period
  • Every property states its next free stretch
  • Arrow keys page the period, and Today brings you back

Pricing

Seasons, discounts, and the price you actually agreed

Write a rule once: peak nights cost more, a week earns ten percent off. The booking dialog then shows the working. If you name a price yourself, it is stored as an agreed figure, and the app stops second guessing you.

  • A season covers a range of nights, not days
  • Discounts do not stack, so a long let cannot triple a rate
  • Rounding is half up on whole minor units, never a float
  • Checking a guest out early is repriced by the same rules

Documents

Paperwork that survives an audit

A tenancy agreement and an invoice, written as PDF files you can print, email, or attach to a tax return. The document number comes from the booking date and the booking itself, so regenerating a document does not produce a different number, and two computers cannot disagree about it.

  • Amounts come from the stored booking, never from current rates
  • Season lines and the discount are printed separately
  • Your company details are filled in once, in Settings
  • The agreement carries a signature block for both parties
